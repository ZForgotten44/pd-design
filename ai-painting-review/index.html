II · The evidence — 08 Art vs. artist
ARTARTIST
Art status

Paintings by humans and by AI-driven robots were judged art to a broadly similar degree.

The landscape-painting art-status contrast was not significant.
Artist status

A larger human advantage — partly explained by attributing less artistic intention to robots.

Two experiments, N = 693 · written scenarios about hypothetical robots · folk judgments, not a test of any real system or of consciousness

Art-making is a social activity of social agents. Current computers are not artists — but human computer art is art.

Criterion: social agency

The verdict depends on the theory: process, outcome or relationships. Human–machine boundaries can hide the networks behind a work.

Criterion: depends on account

Human direction and viewer interpretation can supply purpose and meaning. Evoked emotion is not felt emotion in the model.

Criterion: aesthetic experience
“An artist is whoever creates art” doesn’t settle it — it moves the dispute to who counts as creating.

III · Interpretation — 10 My reading · Mahmoud Hafez

Two answers, not one.

Art statusCan be art
An AI-generated image can count as art — through human artistic practice and viewer engagement.Supported by: art status granted readily (Mikalonytė & Kneer); human selection and editing in practice (Kuta); meaning supplied by direction and interpretation (Chatterjee).
Artist statusNot established
Treating the model as an independent artist is a separate, contested claim the reviewed experiments do not establish.Experiments measure viewers’ ratings and attributions — not a system’s agency (Bellaiche; Horton; van Hees).
Where others would disagree

Outcome-focused or relational theories could grant more to the system, or spread credit across a human–machine network (Coeckelbergh, 2017).

What this is not

An expressive image, or a viewer’s attribution of feeling, is not evidence of machine consciousness — nor proof that it is impossible.

III · Interpretation — 11 Significance

Why this matters beyond the gallery.

01 · Trust
Labels shape how all work is judged.

Attribution changes perceived depth and worth, and can raise or lower human work by comparison.

02 · Whose taste
Narrow samples can privilege some traditions.

37 of 44 included studies used single-country samples; AICAN was trained on a Western art corpus.

03 · Credit
Opaque workflows misplace creative credit.

A single “AI” label hides prompting, selection and editing — and the networks behind a work.
