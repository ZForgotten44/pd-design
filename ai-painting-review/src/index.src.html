Mean rating of the second work, 1–7: a mix of “creative”, “novel”, “liked” and “fit for a gallery sale”. Experiment 4 of six (2,965 participants in total). The paintings shown only illustrate the design.

At an art fair, with two real canvases, the penalty depended on order.

“Human”→“AI”The AI-labelled painting was liked less.
“AI”→“Human”No difference.

Both canvases were human-made abstract paintings; only the label changed. No overall label effect (p = .60); the label mattered only with order (p = .016).

Across many studies, the average penalty is small for sensory and emotional responses and moderate for meaning.

A meta-analysis pools studies like these, so it summarises them rather than adding a replication.

Question 2 · Does art need an artist?
A story, like those participants read

A robot driven by artificial intelligence paints a landscape.

Is the painting art?

Is the robot an artist?

Participants read written stories only; no images. The stories varied who painted, whether it was on purpose, and the style.

ARTARTIST

Participants · the painting

Judged art about as readily as a human’s painting, though this varied across the story versions.

Participants · the robot

Much less often called an artist, partly because people attributed less artistic intention to it.

Philosophers disagree about why the robot isn’t an artist.

Philosopher · social agency

Art is an exchange between social agents. Today’s computers are not artists; people using them make computer art.

Philosopher · it depends on the theory

Judge by outcome, process or relationships, and you get different verdicts. Sharp human–machine lines can hide the network behind a work.

My interpretation

The painting

An AI-generated painting can count as art, through the people who make, select and look at it.

The model

Calling the model an independent artist is a separate claim, and this research does not establish it.

An outcome-focused theory could grant more. And no study shows machine consciousness; an expressive image is not evidence of it.

This drawing was made by code, not a hand. Does that change it for you?

Why it matters, and what to study next

Labels shape trust in human work, who gets credit, and whose taste counts.

Limitation

Studies used older, single models such as Artbreeder and DALL·E 2.

Next question

Do the findings hold across current, versioned models?

Limitation

“Made by AI” hides the human work.

Next question

What changes when the workflow is documented?

Rondini et al., 2026: more human guidance raised the rated creativity of AI images.

Limitation

Narrow audiences, and narrow tools.

Next question

Whose taste do our ratings and scorers encode?

Taylor et al., 2026: a widely used aesthetic scorer favoured realistic landscapes, cities and portraits by Western and Japanese artists.

The field has experiments, reviews and a meta-analysis, but no validated test of artistry across systems and cultures (my reading). These are proposed questions, not predictions.
