III · My interpretation · Mahmoud Hafez
Art status · the image

Can be art

Through human artistic practice and viewer engagement. Art status granted readily (Mikalonytė & Kneer); selection and editing in practice (Kuta); meaning supplied by direction and interpretation (Chatterjee).

Artist status · the model

Not established

A separate, contested claim. The experiments measure viewers’ ratings and attributions, not a system’s agency (Bellaiche; Horton; van Hees).

Where others would disagree: outcome-focused or relational theories could grant more, or spread credit across a human–machine network (Coeckelbergh, 2017).

What this is not: an expressive image, or a viewer’s attribution of feeling, is not evidence of machine consciousness, nor proof that it is impossible.

III · Why it matters

Beyond the gallery.

01 · TRUST
Labels shape how all work is judged.

Attribution moves perceived depth and worth, and can lift or lower human work by comparison.

02 · WHOSE TASTE
Narrow samples can privilege some traditions.

37 of 44 included studies used single-country samples; AICAN trained on a Western corpus.

03 · CREDIT
Opaque workflows misplace credit.

One “AI” label hides prompting, selection and editing, and the network behind a work.

III · Limitations → next steps

What the studies could not show…

…names the next study.

Each card carries its limitation across to the study that would address it. These are proposals, not predictions.

Older single systems · hand-picked outputs · vague labels · composite scales · narrow samples · detection read as creativity → each becomes a design rule for the next study.

Where the field is · my reading, not a formal stage model

Experiments, reviews and a meta-analysis exist. A validated test of artistry across systems and cultures does not.

Forecast · not a measured trend

More studies of the human–AI process and of culturally varied reception than of “human vs. AI” labels alone.
