## What the measures mean (say these in your own words if asked)

**Bellaiche et al. (2023), Study 1.** Every painting was AI-made (Artbreeder); each one randomly carried a "human-created" or "AI-created" label. Each painting was rated 1–5 on four questions, quoted from the paper:
- **Liking:** "How much do you like this image?"
- **Beauty:** "How beautiful/aesthetically pleasing is this image?"
- **Profundity:** "How profound or meaningful is this image?" In other words, how deep it feels.
- **Worth:** "How much money would this work be worth?" Rated 1–5 from "None at all" to "Worth quite a lot".

The bar numbers are effect sizes (Cohen's *d*): how far ratings moved with a "human" label, in standard units. About .2 is small, .5 medium and .8 large. Liking .17 and beauty .22 are small; profundity .47 and worth .61 are medium.

**Horton et al. (2023), Experiment 4 (n = 789).** Everyone saw the same two paintings, one after the other. They rated *each* painting on how creative, novel and likeable it was, and how appropriate it was to sell in a gallery (1–7, averaged into "creativity"). Only the labels changed. The comparison is painting 2's score:
- 4.62 when both paintings said "human" (the control);
- 4.24 when painting 2 said "AI";
- 4.85 when painting 1 said "AI" and painting 2 said "human".

**"First" and "second"** simply mean the order the two paintings were shown in.

**Chiarella et al. (2022).** Visitors at an art fair saw two real human-made canvases (Abstract 12 and Abstract 18), one at a time:
1. a minute of rest;
2. a minute in front of the covered canvas;
3. the label ("human" or "AI");
4. a minute looking at the painting;
5. a rating of "I really like this painting" (1–5).

Then the same steps for the second painting.

**van Hees et al. (2025).** 50 pairs, each a lesser-known work by a famous painter and a DALL·E 2 image matched by artist and genre:
- Group 1 chose which they preferred.
- Group 2 chose which was AI.

In the scatter plot, x is how often group 1 preferred the AI image and y how often group 2 spotted it; 50 is chance. r = .42 is a moderate positive correlation.

**Rondini et al. (2026).** Everyone completed the same two starting lines (45 s to think, 120 s to draw). 255 raters scored the results 1–7 on liking, vividness, originality, aesthetics and curiosity.
